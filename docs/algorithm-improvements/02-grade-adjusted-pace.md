## Executive verdict

The current grade-adjusted pace (GAP) implementation is a useful prototype, not a validated arrival-time model. It takes a published treadmill metabolic-cost polynomial, assumes constant metabolic power, converts cost directly to a speed multiplier, applies two product-authored clamps, smooths elevation over three skeleton intervals, and integrates time. This creates plausible-looking hills and makes playback legible, but the output is more certain than the evidence supports.

The most consequential problem is a category jump: **metabolic energy cost per meter is not observed outdoor pacing**. The code assumes runners hold constant metabolic power, yet self-paced hill experiments report that they do not. Terrain, downhill skill and caution, effort intent, fatigue, route duration, surface, altitude, and elevation quality are absent. The user enters an “expected flat-ground pace,” but the UI does not establish whether that means easy effort, race effort, recent average, or a pace already measured on rolling terrain.

The arbitrary downhill cap is especially blunt. With the current polynomial, `MAX_SPEED_FACTOR = 1.10` takes effect at about -2% grade, so every descent from roughly -2% through -30% predicts exactly the same 10% speedup. The uphill minimum similarly overrides the cited model near the steep end. Those choices may be defensible safety priors, but they are neither calibrated nor represented as uncertainty.

The recommended target is a versioned pace-model interface that separates (1) an elevation/grade estimate, (2) runner effort intent and flat reference speed, (3) a population outdoor grade-response curve, (4) optional conservative personalization, and (5) an error-controlled time integrator. Ship uncertainty and low-confidence fallbacks before adding more variables. Validate against held-out activities and actual landmark/finish arrival times, with the current model and flat pace as mandatory baselines.

## Current algorithm

For grade `i` as a rise/run fraction, `gap.ts` evaluates:

```text
C(i) = 155.4 i^5 - 30.4 i^4 - 43.3 i^3 + 46.3 i^2 + 19.5 i + 3.6
raw speed factor = C(0) / C(i) = 3.6 / C(i)
grade input = clamp(i, -0.30, +0.30)
speed factor = clamp(raw factor, 0.35, 1.10)
```

`buildSkeleton` then:

1. creates up to 500 equally spaced route samples, targeting 50 m;
2. estimates interval grade from the elevation difference across a centered window of up to three intervals;
3. treats the user's speed as flat-ground speed;
4. integrates each interval with left-endpoint grade:

```text
dt[k] = intervalDistance / (flatSpeed * gradeSpeedFactor(grade[k]))
arrival[k + 1] = arrival[k] + dt[k]
```

Time complexity is O(M) after geometry sampling and memory is O(M), where M <= 500. The formula itself is O(1). This is operationally excellent; the weaknesses are model validity, elevation quality, resolution dependence, and missing uncertainty.

## Strengths worth preserving

- The implementation is deterministic, transparent, tiny, portable, and easy to version.
- Flat ground is exactly the user's entered speed, preserving an understandable anchor.
- Integrating interval time is structurally correct; hill effects propagate to later weather, sun, wind, alerts, playback, and splits rather than changing only a displayed finish time.
- Grade and speed clamps ensure finite positive times for finite valid inputs.
- Physical-distance smoothing is directionally better than differencing noisy raw GPX points one by one.
- Tests cover flat identity, uphill monotonicity, downhill capping, slower round trips, monotone arrival, speed scaling, downstream time propagation, and time-distance inversion.
- The code comments explicitly acknowledge treadmill-to-outdoor limitations rather than presenting Minetti as ground truth.

The target should keep a pure, inspectable model and exact version reproducibility. Personalization must not become an unexplainable black box.

## What the evidence does and does not support

Minetti et al.'s [Energy cost of walking and running at extreme uphill and downhill slopes](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.01177.2001) is valid evidence for metabolic energy cost in its experiment: ten runners on a treadmill over gradients from -0.45 to +0.45. It is a valuable physiological benchmark. It does **not** by itself validate `speed = flatSpeed * C(0)/C(i)` for an arbitrary runner planning an outdoor run. Its downhill results and discussion of safety/neuromusculoskeletal limits reinforce that descent behavior is not only metabolic cost.

Staab et al.'s self-paced treadmill races, [Metabolic and performance responses to uphill and downhill running in distance runners](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1548986/), found that runners changed pace inversely with grade but did not maintain constant energy expenditure; +5%/-5% hilly courses were slower than flat despite equal ascent and descent. This directly challenges the current constant-power conversion as a universal pacing rule, while supporting the product policy that symmetric hills should usually impose net time cost.

Townshend et al.'s [Spontaneous pacing during overground hill running](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20010117/) measured physiology, GPS speed, and mechanics in actual overground hill running. It supports validating the product in the field rather than assuming treadmill economy transfers exactly.

Research on [level, uphill and downhill running economy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30357515/) found strong correlations across modes in trained runners but still attributes inter-individual differences to physiology and skill. Newer work on [running grade aptitude](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40812385/) reports person-specific uphill/downhill economy associations. These studies justify conservative personalization as a later phase; they do not justify learning a highly flexible individual curve from a handful of noisy GPS runs.

## Product definition before model work

The team must define the forecasted quantity. Recommended initial contract:

> Given a runner's sustainable flat-ground speed for this run's intended effort and duration, estimate median outdoor arrival time along this route under ordinary runnable-surface behavior, with an uncertainty range driven by elevation and model confidence.

This intentionally avoids claiming race-optimal pacing or physiological power. Add an `effortIntent` such as `easy`, `steady`, or `race` only after data demonstrates distinct, predictable response curves. Until then, ask for “expected flat pace for this run” and explain that a pace taken from a hilly route may double-count hills.

Do not silently reinterpret existing saved `speed` values. Persist pace-semantics/model version and migrate UX copy separately from the algorithm.

## Recommended target architecture

```text
PlannableRouteV2
  -> ElevationProfile { elevation, source, confidence, resolution }
  -> GradeProfile { interval grade distribution/confidence }

PaceContext
  { flatReferenceSpeed, referenceSemantics, effortIntent?, routeDistance,
    surface?, runnerModel?, environmentalContext? }

GradePaceModel(version)
  evaluate(context, gradeInterval, state)
  -> { medianSpeedFactor, uncertainty, reasons, nextState }

AdaptiveTimeIntegrator
  -> arrival-time knots + finish P50/range + confidence + diagnostics
  -> environmental evaluation and presentation meshes
```

### 1. Stable elevation and grade profile

Grade calculation must be independent of the 500-point environmental skeleton.

- Resample/fuse elevation in physical distance at a resolution justified by source resolution; never create detail finer than the source supports.
- Smooth with a robust local method whose bandwidth is measured in meters. Candidate approaches include robust LOESS/local polynomial regression, a Savitzky-Golay filter on a uniform grid, or a state-space smoother that accepts source uncertainty.
- Preserve important crests/valleys and total ascent while suppressing isolated spikes. Evaluate filters against surveyed/barometric profiles; do not pick one because its chart looks smooth.
- Represent grade over intervals and uncertainty, not only a point value. Grade should use horizontal along-route run, with consistent distance/elevation datums.
- Segment boundaries and missing gaps must not be bridged.

### 2. Versioned pace-model interface

Extract `gradeSpeedFactor` behind an interface before changing its behavior. Keep the current function as `minetti-clamped-v1` for exact replay. Every output should include model version and active constraints/reasons.

Recommended first replacement: a monotone/shape-constrained population curve fitted to outdoor data, with separate uphill and downhill branches and smooth derivatives at flat ground. Use conservative bounded extrapolation outside well-supported grades. A shape-constrained spline or low-degree basis is preferable to an unconstrained neural model: it can be inspected, versioned, and kept positive.

Required qualitative constraints should be product/science decisions, not accidental polynomial properties:

- exact or calibrated continuity at 0%;
- positive finite speeds;
- non-increasing speed with increasing uphill grade over the supported running range;
- downhill benefit can peak and then diminish as technical/braking demands rise rather than plateau forever;
- extreme grades may transition to a walk/hike model if that is in product scope;
- bounds are smooth and empirically justified, with unsupported ranges marked low-confidence.

### 3. Effort policy is separate from terrain response

A population curve describes typical observed pacing conditional on an effort definition. It should not bury a universal constant-power assumption.

Start with one documented “steady expected effort” policy. Evaluate later alternatives:

- **Even metabolic effort:** useful for easy-run planning, but requires physiology/power estimates and is contradicted as a description of all self-paced racing.
- **Observed typical pacing:** best initial product fit; learn outdoor grade response from comparable runs.
- **Race-optimal pacing:** a constrained optimization over critical power/speed, duration, fatigue, and course; potentially valuable but a separate feature with much more input and validation burden.
- **Even pace:** user-directed scenario, not a physiological prediction.

Expose the policy in UX if multiple policies exist. Do not let the same pace input mean different things based on an invisible model version.

### 4. Conservative personalization

Personalization should shrink toward the population curve and activate only after sufficient, diverse, high-quality observations.

A practical hierarchical model could estimate `log(observed speed / estimated flat effort speed)` as a smooth function of grade with runner-specific uphill/downhill deviations, while controlling for surface, wind, temperature, activity duration/fatigue position, altitude, stop probability, and measurement quality. Use robust losses or an explicit outlier component.

Critical safeguards:

- split train/validation by whole activity and preferably route, not random segments, to prevent leakage;
- exclude stops, intersections, GPS jumps, warm-up/cool-down, races with unknown maximal intent, and low-confidence elevation;
- require grade coverage on both directions before learning asymmetry;
- cap personalization toward a population prior and publish a data-sufficiency score;
- give users deletion/opt-out controls and never infer sensitive health traits unnecessarily;
- do not train the model on its own planned values or on route segments whose grade came from the same corrupt source being evaluated.

### 5. Error-controlled time integration

Integrate `dt = ds / v(s)` on grade-profile breakpoints with midpoint or adaptive quadrature. Refine until duration change is below tolerance, not until a global point cap is hit. Emit arrival-time knots sufficient for weather/sun interpolation; the rendering mesh can remain capped separately.

If the model is probabilistic, avoid falsely treating interval errors as independent. A runner who is generally slower than forecast will be correlated across the whole route. Initial uncertainty can combine route-level pace error, uphill/downhill response error, and elevation scenarios through a small deterministic ensemble/Monte Carlo with fixed seeds. Validate interval coverage rather than merely displaying +/- an arbitrary percent.

## Data and calibration strategy

### Ground truth hierarchy

1. **Highest value:** consented outdoor activities with reliable timestamps, high-quality horizontal tracks, barometric or trusted elevation, and known effort/run type.
2. **Useful validation:** official race split/checkpoint times and surveyed courses, linked with consent.
3. **Physiological prior:** controlled treadmill/overground studies such as Minetti and Townshend.
4. **Weak labels:** ordinary consumer GPX activities without effort/surface/elevation quality. Use cautiously and down-weight.

Planned route geometry, Strava/provider GAP values, and the current Runcast model are not ground truth.

### Dataset design

- Define a privacy and consent model before collecting activities. Retain only what is needed; separate identity from modeling records.
- Stratify by grade, ascent/descent length, route distance, base pace, surface/technicality, altitude, climate, accumulated fatigue, device/source, and runner experience.
- Seek representative coverage rather than allowing high-volume competitive trail runners to define all users.
- Label stops and intersections. Use map context only where licensing and quality permit.
- Maintain immutable dataset/model cards with collection dates, exclusions, feature definitions, known gaps, and subgroup counts.
- Freeze a never-trained-on field test set and a small human-reviewed failure corpus.

### Fitting and evaluation

- Predict interval travel time or log speed factor with weights by usable distance and measurement confidence.
- Compare at minimum against (a) no grade adjustment and (b) `minetti-clamped-v1`.
- Use nested/temporal validation for model selection, with final splits by athlete and route as appropriate to the claimed generalization.
- Report median absolute finish-time error, finish-time MAPE, landmark arrival MAE, signed bias, interval calibration, and recommendation-winner stability.
- Slice metrics by grade bins, ascent/descent, route length, pace, surface, elevation quality, and source. Aggregate improvement cannot excuse a severe downhill or beginner-runner regression.
- Run ablations for every extra feature. Wind/heat/fatigue belongs in pace timing only if it improves held-out timing without double-counting other Runcast components.
- Estimate bootstrap confidence intervals on metric differences. Choose rollout thresholds before looking at production experiment results.

## Staged implementation plan

### Phase 0 — make V1 explicit and measurable

- Rename/version current semantics conceptually as `minetti-clamped-v1`; preserve exact replay.
- Freeze the target estimand, pace-reference semantics, and activity-label protocol before collecting/fitting V2 data. Require a prospectively entered reference pace, a separate flat trial, or a predeclared estimation method that cannot leak target-route travel time.
- Add a scientific benchmark test for the published polynomial separate from tests for Runcast's clamps and round-trip policy.
- Instrument grade/elevation quality, factor-cap occupancy, duration contribution by grade bin, nominal smoothing width, and flat-vs-V1 timing delta.
- Audit the pace UX and persisted `speed` semantics across mobile, web, watches, API jobs, and snapshots.
- Establish flat and V1 benchmark metrics on the initial held-out corpus.

### Phase 1 — trustworthy grade profile and integration

- Depend on `PlannableRouteV2` elevation status/provenance from Algorithm 1; add flat fallback for absent/low-confidence profiles.
- Build and validate a physical-distance grade profile independent of skeleton size.
- Add the `GradePaceModel` interface, model/version metadata, interval weights, and adaptive integrator.
- Run the exact V1 response through the new profile/integrator in shadow mode. This isolates numerical/profile changes from model changes.

### Phase 2 — population outdoor V2

- Fit several simple shape-constrained candidates and a transparent piecewise baseline against the Phase 0 estimand/reference-speed contract.
- Select by predeclared held-out timing/calibration criteria, not visual curve appeal.
- Add a confidence model based on grade support, elevation quality, route context, and empirical residuals.
- Shadow-compute V1/V2 plans and recommendation winners; conduct field review on large deltas.

### Phase 3 — product UX and uncertainty

- Ship the already-defined “expected flat pace for this run” semantics in pace controls and onboarding; avoid migrating saved speed values silently.
- Display finish-time range/confidence and a concise reason when elevation is unavailable or a route is outside supported grade/surface data.
- Propagate timing ensembles/sensitivity into weather and recommendation stability. If candidate rankings overlap materially, say “similar” rather than selecting a falsely precise winner.

### Phase 4 — guarded personalization and richer context

- Train a hierarchical personalized variant only for consented users with sufficient high-quality grade coverage.
- Run population and personalized models side-by-side; activate only when held-out personal error improves and calibration remains acceptable.
- Add surface, technicality, fatigue, environmental pace effects, or walk transitions one at a time through ablation and safety review.
- Keep a one-tap reset/opt-out and population fallback.

## Verification strategy

### Unit and policy tests

- Published polynomial values at selected grades match the cited equation within floating-point tolerance.
- V1 clamp tests name the behavior as Runcast policy, including the exact grade where each cap first binds.
- Every supported model returns finite positive speed and finite uncertainty for finite valid inputs.
- Flat response satisfies the declared reference-speed contract.
- Uphill response is continuous and monotone where the selected model promises it; downhill response follows its declared peak/decline constraints rather than an assumed universal monotonic rule.
- Invalid grade, speed, elevation confidence, and unsupported-context inputs produce typed fallback/error results.
- Model serialization and version replay are deterministic.

### Numerical/property tests

- Arrival times are strictly increasing for positive-length intervals.
- Halving reference speed doubles duration only for models explicitly scale-invariant; otherwise test the declared speed interaction.
- Constant-grade analytic cases match numerical integration within tolerance.
- Refining elevation and integration grids converges; duration does not jump at the old 500-point threshold.
- Splitting one interval into collinear/equal-grade subintervals does not change duration beyond numeric tolerance.
- Reversal preserves duration on flat routes. On non-flat routes, a stateless asymmetric uphill/downhill curve may change duration because every signed grade reverses; stateful/fatigue models add a separately tested order dependence.
- `distanceAtTime` round-trips all arrival knots and remains monotone under uncertainty scenarios.

### Adversarial tests

- All-missing, partially missing, isolated-zero, spiky, stair-step, quantized, noisy, and mixed-datum elevation.
- Very short hills, alternating sawtooth grades, long sustained climbs, crest/valley at endpoints, +/-30% boundaries, and grades beyond the supported range.
- Zero/negative/NaN/infinite speed and grade; zero-distance route; duplicate geometry; discontinuous segments.
- Smooth road descent versus low-confidence/technical descent context; unsupported context must widen confidence or fall back, not hallucinate precision.
- Routes long enough to exceed every legacy sample threshold.

### Golden and scientific benchmarks

- Pin the Minetti equation independently from Runcast conversion/clamps, with citation and units in the fixture metadata.
- Create synthetic grade-profile goldens with hand-computable duration.
- Maintain reviewed actual-activity goldens with elevation source, effort label, checkpoint/finish times, exclusions, and consent lineage.
- Test V1 and V2 on the same frozen corpus and store metric reports as build artifacts, not thousands of brittle per-sample snapshots.
- Include known counterexamples where constant-power or downhill-cap behavior fails; a regression suite should preserve the fix, not the old output.

### Field validation

- Recruit a small structured cohort to run flat, uphill-first, downhill-first, rolling, and smooth/technical variants at a stated effort on surveyed routes.
- Collect checkpoint times, perceived effort, stops, surface notes, and elevation quality. Where appropriate, collect heart rate/power as explanatory signals, not unquestioned effort truth.
- Repeat some runs to estimate within-runner day-to-day noise; model error cannot be interpreted without this floor.
- Review recommendation changes caused by timing differences near forecast/solar boundaries.

## Telemetry and observability

Collect only consented/privacy-reviewed, versioned aggregates:

- pace-model, elevation-profile, canonical-route, and engine versions;
- route distance/ascent/descent/grade/elevation-confidence buckets;
- percentage of distance and time in grade bins;
- uphill/downhill cap or extrapolation occupancy;
- V1/V2 finish delta, P50/range width, recommendation-winner/ranking delta;
- flat fallback rate and reason; unsupported-context rate;
- plan latency, integration refinements, convergence-limit hits, and non-finite/fallback errors;
- when actual activity feedback is explicitly available: signed finish/landmark error and interval coverage, bucketed and access-controlled.

Do not log raw tracks, exact start/finish, route name, fine-grained split traces, health signals, or per-runner coefficients to general application logs. Model-training records need separate consent, retention, deletion, and access controls. Monitor drift by model version and data slice, especially downhill, long routes, low-confidence elevation, and pace bands.

## Rollout and migration

- Persist `paceModelVersion`, `paceReferenceSemantics`, `elevationProfileVersion/status`, `integrationVersion`, and uncertainty method in plans/snapshots.
- Keep V1 exact replay. Model/version changes must bump `ENGINE_VERSION` and all recommendation/cache/idempotency keys affected by semantics.
- Deploy new grade profile/integrator with V1 curve first, then the V2 curve. Combining both in one rollout makes regressions untraceable.
- Shadow-run on synthetic/demo and privacy-approved production routes. Gate by elevation confidence and supported context.
- Canary population V2 without rewriting users' saved speeds. Provide a remote rollback to V1 by plan/model version.
- Existing all-zero elevations are ambiguous: sea-level is valid. Do not “repair” them as missing without original source/provenance. Re-import or retain low-confidence legacy behavior.
- Recompute cached weather sampling, finish windows, alerts, recommendations, watches, splits, and playback when timing changes. Route geometry need not change for these caches to become stale.
- Communicate meaningful finish-time changes and avoid presenting old/new plans as directly comparable without model labels.

## Acceptance criteria

Pre-register the final numeric thresholds against a frozen validation corpus. The numbers below are provisional hypotheses, not evidence-based commitments; Phase 0 must replace or ratify them before model selection. Finish-time percentage error uses actual moving duration as its denominator, and every sliced gate needs a predeclared minimum sample size.

### Safety and correctness

- 0 non-finite, non-positive-speed, or non-monotone arrival outputs across unit, generative, adversarial, corpus, and shadow tests.
- Missing/low-confidence elevation always produces the documented flat/bounded fallback and visible confidence reason; it never enters grade arithmetic as zero.
- Integration duration differs by <= 0.25% when the grade/integration resolution is doubled for 99% of supported-corpus routes and <= 0.5% for all reviewed supported routes.
- Unsupported grades/contexts use bounded extrapolation or typed fallback and cannot silently hit an unexplained hard plateau.
- Exact V1 artifacts remain reproducible by `minetti-clamped-v1`.

### Predictive quality

- On held-out outdoor activities, V2 reduces median absolute finish-time percentage error by at least 20% versus V1 and at least 10% versus flat pace. If V1 is already within the repeatability floor in a slice, require non-inferiority rather than artificial improvement.
- Signed finish-time bias is within +/-1% overall and +/-3% in each predeclared supported grade/distance/surface slice with adequate sample size.
- Landmark arrival MAE improves or is non-inferior to both baselines; a finish-only gain cannot hide badly timed intermediate hills that corrupt weather sampling.
- The advertised 80% timing interval contains 75-85% of held-out outcomes overall and no adequately sized critical slice is outside 70-90%.
- No predeclared supported cohort/slice regresses median absolute finish error by >5% relative to V1 without explicit review and documented product rationale.

### Product and operations

- P95 grade-profile plus timing integration remains below 10 ms on the supported API tier and within a separately recorded interactive budget on representative low-end mobile hardware.
- Shadow/canary recommendation winners change only with an explainable timing delta; every severe-alert window change is manually sampled and reviewed before expansion.
- Users can understand the reference pace and confidence state in usability testing; fewer than a predeclared threshold interpret the input as already hill-adjusted.
- Personalization activates only after its held-out personal metric beats population V2 by a predeclared margin and never worsens calibrated interval coverage beyond the gate.
- Rollback restores V1 without data loss, and all outputs are attributable to route/elevation/pace/integration versions.

## Risks and open decisions

1. **What does entered pace mean?** Flat sustainable speed for this intended run is recommended, but existing user expectations and saved defaults need research.
2. **Forecast or prescription?** Typical arrival forecast, constant-effort scenario, and race-optimal pacing are different products. V2 must choose one.
3. **What elevation quality is sufficient?** Define source-specific confidence and the threshold for adjustment versus flat fallback.
4. **Can the project collect actual activities with informed consent?** Without representative field data, a significantly better universal outdoor curve cannot be claimed.
5. **Which surfaces are in scope?** Road, track, smooth trail, technical trail, stairs, and scrambling need different evidence. Unsupported contexts should be explicit.
6. **Is walking on steep grades in scope?** A walk/run transition is more realistic for steep trails but requires user intent and a distinct model.
7. **How should timing uncertainty affect recommendations?** A single best start may be misleading when candidates are statistically indistinguishable.
8. **What minimum data enables personalization?** Decide with learning curves and held-out performance, not an arbitrary activity count.
9. **Which environmental variables alter pace versus comfort only?** Adding wind/heat to both can double-count unless timing and comfort responsibilities are explicit.
10. **How much model complexity is operationally acceptable?** On-device portability, auditability, reproducibility, privacy deletion, and rollback are hard constraints.

## Recommended decision

Do not tune `MIN_SPEED_FACTOR`, `MAX_SPEED_FACTOR`, or the three-interval window in isolation and call the result a better GAP model. Approve Phase 0, elevation/grade-profile decoupling, and the versioned model interface first. Treat Minetti as a transparent V1 benchmark. Build V2 from consented outdoor arrival data with a simple shape-constrained population curve, calibrated uncertainty, and explicit pace semantics; add personalization and fatigue/terrain context only after they beat that baseline on held-out routes.
